What is path planning? Path planning is computing a collision free sequence of motions that takes a robot from a start configuration to a goal configuration. Notice the words: sequence of motions, not just a line on a map; collision free, respecting the robot's body and obstacles; and configuration, the full description of the robot's position and joints. Path planning is where geometry, algorithms, and robotics meet.

Let me distinguish related terms. A path is a geometric curve in configuration space without timing. A trajectory is a path with time: velocities and accelerations along it. Navigation, which you just studied, uses path planning as one module among several. Motion planning is the broader term including dynamics and constraints. PART 2 - PROBLEM FORMULATION AND CONFIGURATION SPACE

Let me walk you through formulation and configuration space, the foundation of all planning.

A path planning problem is defined by: the robot's configuration space, the start configuration, the goal region, the obstacle region, and the cost function. The planner finds a continuous path in free space from start to goal.

Configuration space construction. For a planar disc robot, obstacles inflate by the robot's radius; the robot becomes a point. For a robot with rotation, the space includes heading; obstacles grow into three dimensional shapes. For a two joint arm, the space is a square of joint angles with obstacle regions computed from workspace geometry. The mapping from workspace to configuration space is the masterstroke of planning theory.

Free space may be disconnected: no path exists; planners must detect and report this honestly.

Cost functions: path length, clearance from obstacles, energy, or smoothness; optimal planning minimizes cost; satisficing finds acceptable paths quickly.

Completeness and resolution completeness: will the planner find a path if one exists; sampling planners are probabilistically complete: success probability approaches one with more samples.

Dimensionality curse: spaces grow fast with degrees of freedom; this shapes algorithm choice across the course.

Real life practicals. A Nigerian student computes C obstacles for a rotating square robot on paper and visualizes free space. An arm planning engineer models a seven joint space for a factory arm. A drone planner works in three dimensional space with no fly volumes. Formulate first; the formulation reveals the algorithm family.

A practical habit: for every planning problem, sketch the configuration space for a simplified case before coding; geometric understanding prevents algorithm misuse.

PART 3 - GRID BASED PLANNING

Let me walk you through grid based planning: the reliable workhorses.

Grid methods discretize configuration space into cells. Breadth first search finds shortest paths in step count; Dijkstra finds least cost paths; A star finds least cost paths with heuristics, exploring far less with good guidance.

Heuristic design for A star. Manhattan distance for four connected grids; Euclidean for eight connected; and true distance measures when costs vary. Admissible heuristics never overestimate, preserving optimality; consistent heuristics keep search smooth.

Cost grids: cells carry traversal costs; planners respect costs naturally. Weighted A star trades optimality for speed; anytime A star improves solutions over time.

D star and D star Lite handle changing maps: incremental replanning from previous solutions as obstacles appear; essential for robots that learn about the world while moving.

Jump point search and theta star speed grid search by exploiting grid structure and allowing any angle paths.

Grid limitations: resolution trade offs; memory at high dimensions; and discretization errors at boundaries.

Real life practicals. A Nigerian logistics robot runs D star Lite on a live cost map, re planning around suddenly appearing pallets. A student implements A star with Manhattan and Euclidean heuristics and compares expansions. A game studio uses jump point search for unit navigation in real time. Grid planning is the dependable baseline every engineer should master.

A practical habit: measure nodes expanded and planning time for every algorithm variant; intuition about efficiency needs numbers.

PART 4 - ROADMAP AND SAMPLING BASED PLANNING

Let me walk you through roadmaps and sampling: the answers to high dimensionality.

Roadmap methods build a graph of collision free configurations and connections, then search it. Visibility graphs connect configurations that see each other; shortest paths hug obstacle corners. Voronoi diagrams keep maximal clearance; ideal for cautious navigation. These classical methods are exact for low dimensions.

Probabilistic roadmaps sample random free configurations, connect nearby samples with local planners, and query paths by graph search. Their power: high dimensional spaces handled gracefully; multi query efficiency for repeated planning in the same space; and probabilistic completeness.

Sampling strategies matter. Uniform sampling covers space but wastes samples in obstacles; obstacle informed sampling biases toward interesting regions; bridge test sampling finds narrow passages; and low discrepancy sequences cover space evenly.

Connection rules: connect k nearest neighbors or all within radius; local planners check collision along straight or simple paths; narrow passages need special handling with bridge tests and medial axis sampling.

Rapidly exploring random trees grow from start, extending toward random targets with nearest neighbor connections; bidirectional variants grow from both start and goal; RRT star rewires for asymptotic optimality.

Informed sampling and pruning for optimality: focus sampling in ellipsoids of potentially improving paths.

Real life practicals. A Nigerian robotics lab plans seven joint arm motions with probabilistic roadmaps for machine tending. A student implements RRT in Python and visualizes tree growth. A drone planner uses RRT star to plan smooth energy efficient routes. Sampling conquers dimensionality.

A practical habit: visualize your samples and connections; coverage pictures reveal sampling problems instantly.

PART 5 - POTENTIAL FIELDS AND OPTIMIZATION PLANNING

Let me walk you through potential fields and optimization: continuous approaches to planning.

Potential field methods treat the goal as attracting and obstacles as repelling the robot; gradient descent follows the field to the goal. Elegant and fast, they suffer local minima: equilibrium points away from the goal where forces balance.

Local minima escapes: random walks, harmonic potentials without minima, navigation functions crafted to be minima free, and combinations with grid planning to escape traps.

Optimization based planning frames path finding as minimizing cost subject to constraints: smoothness, collision avoidance, and dynamic limits. Trajectory optimization refines initial guesses into good paths with numerical methods: gradient descent, sequential quadratic programming, and random shooting.

Chomp and trajopt style methods smooth and optimize paths in environments with obstacles; differentiable collision costs enable gradient based refinement.

Model predictive style planning rolls optimization horizons; replanning each step handles disturbances.

Learning from optimization: warm starts from previous solutions speed re planning.

Real life practicals. A Nigerian drone team optimizes smooth trajectories for inspection flights around towers. A student implements a potential field controller and hunts local minima with random restarts. A robot arm uses trajectory optimization to smooth a rough planned path. Optimize where smoothness and constraints matter.

A practical habit: always check optimized paths for constraint satisfaction after refinement; numerical methods can violate limits near boundaries.

PART 6 - KINODYNAMIC, ARTICULATED, AND CONSTRAINED PLANNING

Let me walk you through planning with dynamics, articulation, and constraints.

Kinodynamic planning respects velocity, acceleration, and torque limits; not every geometric path is executable. State space includes positions and velocities; controls drive the system; planners must respect dynamics exactly or conservatively.

Kinodynamic RRT extends sampling to dynamics; motion primitives, short dynamically feasible segments, become the actions; and hybrid planning separates geometric paths from dynamic execution where valid.

Planning for articulated systems: robot arms with many joints, mobile manipulators, and closed chains. High dimensional sampling with task space heuristics guides search.

Task and motion planning integrates symbolic tasks with geometric feasibility, which you met in AI 311; here the geometric side receives deeper treatment.

Constrained planning: keeping a cup level while moving, maintaining tool orientation, and respecting joint limits; constraints restrict free space into manifolds; planners must navigate constrained spaces with specialized sampling.

Planning with contacts: pushing, bracing, and legged locomotion involve contact sequences; contact planning is the frontier of manipulation and locomotion planning.

Real life practicals. A Nigerian factory plans kinodynamically for a high speed pick and place arm respecting torque limits. A student plans with a level constraint for a waiter robot carrying drinks. A mobile manipulator plans base and arm motions jointly for door opening. Respect dynamics and constraints in planning, or execution will fail.

A practical habit: validate planned paths with a dynamics simulation before hardware; feasibility surprises are cheaper in simulation.

PART 7 - MULTI ROBOT PLANNING, UNCERTAINTY, AND REPLANNING

Let me walk you through planning for teams and under uncertainty.

Multi robot path planning computes collision free paths for several robots. Prioritized planning plans robots in sequence, avoiding earlier planned robots; simple but incomplete in some cases. Conflict based search plans independently and resolves collisions by adding constraints iteratively; efficient and complete in many practical cases. Windowed approaches re plan continuously for large fleets.

Coordination spaces model joint motions exactly for small teams; scaling demands decoupled approaches.

Planning under uncertainty: probabilistic outcomes, sensor noise, and moving obstacles. Contingent plans branch on observations; policies prescribe actions per belief; chance constrained planning bounds risk probabilities.

Anytime and incremental planning: return good paths quickly and improve with computation; essential for real time systems.

Replanning architectures: plan, execute, monitor, and re plan on events; re planning strategies balance responsiveness against computation.

Real life practicals. A Nigerian warehouse uses conflict based search with priority rules for its robot fleet. A delivery robot re plans around pedestrians with rolling horizon planning. A rescue fleet uses windowed planning across a large site. Plan for teams and contingencies; static single plans are for textbooks.

A practical habit: stress test planners with adversarial scenarios: narrow passages, symmetric maps, and dynamic blockages.

PART 8 - HEURISTICS, IMPLEMENTATION, EVALUATION, AND LEARNING

Let me close with heuristic design, implementation practice, evaluation, learning, and applications.

Heuristic design deepens. Pattern databases store exact costs of abstractions as heuristics; landmarks provide must pass waypoints with admissible estimates; and learning predicts heuristic values from data while checking admissibility when needed.

Implementation practice: use proven libraries for production; implement from scratch for learning; profile bottlenecks; use efficient data structures like heaps and KD trees; and parallelize where possible.

Evaluation and benchmarking: metrics: planning time, path cost, success rate, and anytime behavior; benchmark suites standardize comparisons; and honest reporting requires distributions, not single runs.

Learning augmented planning: learned heuristics speed search; learned value functions guide sampling; imitation from human paths; and learned costmaps from experience. Learned components retain guarantees through validation layers.

Applications recap: logistics routing, arm motion planning, drone route planning, field coverage, exploration planning, and game AI. For Nigeria and Africa: delivery logistics on complex compounds, farm coverage with battery limits, infrastructure inspection routes, and evacuation planning support.

PART 9 - ADVANCED TOPICS IN PATH PLANNING

Let me deepen your mastery with advanced topics that distinguish expert planners.

Optimal planning theory. The shortest path in a polygonal environment hugs obstacle vertices; visibility graphs capture this exactly. Continuous Dijkstra propagates wavefronts through free space computing exact geodesics; fast marching methods approximate them efficiently on grids. Understanding exact solutions in simple worlds grounds your use of approximations in complex ones.

Planning on manifolds. Constraints like keeping a tray level leave the robot on a lower dimensional manifold within configuration space; sampling on manifolds uses projection and tangent space exploration; and task space regions specify goal sets like all poses placing the peg above the hole.

Decomposition methods. Cell decomposition partitions free space into simple regions connected into graphs; exact for low dimensions. Hierarchical decomposition plans coarsely then refines, saving computation for large worlds.

Replanning theory. D star Lite updates shortest paths with obstacle changes using previous search results; Lifelong Planning A star shares updates across goals; and experience graphs warm start searches from past solutions. For robots operating for hours in changing worlds, incremental methods keep plans fresh at low cost.

Planning with time windows and schedules. When tasks have time constraints, paths must respect schedules; space time planning treats time as another dimension; and reservation based methods coordinate fleets with schedules.

Adversarial and competitive planning. When other agents respond to your robot, game theoretic planning anticipates their reactions; simple avoidance assumes others do not respond strategically.

Numerical robustness. Floating point geometry errors cause subtle failures; robust predicates, exact arithmetic where needed, and careful tolerance handling distinguish production planners from fragile ones.

Real life practicals. A Nigerian drone planner uses fast marching methods to compute smooth geodesics over terrain cost fields. A student implements cell decomposition for a polygonal world and compares with sampling methods. A warehouse vendor adopts experience graphs so repeated routes plan instantly. Depth in theory makes practice reliable.

A practical habit: when a planner fails, simplify the world until it works, then reintroduce complexity step by step; this isolates causes efficiently.

Let me extend your planning vocabulary and prepare for assessment. Additional terms. Homotopy class: family of paths continuously deformable into each other. Random restart: re beginning search to escape traps. Meta heuristic: high level search strategy. Parameter sweep: systematic parameter exploration. Robustness test: performance under perturbation. Regression suite: tests preventing re introduced failures. Baseline comparison: measuring against standard methods. Profile: measurement of time by component.
